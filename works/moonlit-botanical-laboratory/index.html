Prompt
プロンプト全文

3モデルへ渡した共通のプロンプトです。

Create a complete, visually rich pixel-art animation of a **magical botanical laboratory**.

The goal is to create a scene that feels immediately beautiful, alive, cozy, mysterious, and worth watching for a long time.

This is a **one-shot generation task**. Make all creative and technical decisions yourself. Do not ask questions. Do not provide an explanation before building it.

## World

Create a magical botanist’s laboratory hidden somewhere between a greenhouse, an alchemist’s workshop, and a wizard’s study.

The scene should contain many universally recognizable visual elements:

- plants
- flowers
- vines
- mushrooms
- glass bottles
- jars
- books
- water
- fire or candlelight
- moonlight
- stars
- insects
- a cat
- wooden furniture
- windows
- shelves
- hanging herbs
- small tools
- glowing objects

The room should feel dense with details, but compositionally clear rather than cluttered.

The atmosphere should feel warm and inviting inside, with a mysterious magical world outside.

## Composition

Build the scene with strong visual depth.

Use multiple layers such as:

- foreground objects partially framing the scene
- the main laboratory workspace in the middle
- shelves, windows, plants, and architecture behind it
- a visible exterior world beyond the window

The viewer should immediately understand the space.

Include one strong visual focal point, such as a large magical plant, glowing botanical specimen, strange flower, or experimental terrarium in the center of the laboratory.

Around it, distribute many smaller details that reward closer inspection.

## Pixel-art direction

The entire scene must look intentionally designed as pixel art.

Use:

- crisp pixel edges
- limited but expressive color palettes
- controlled dithering
- clusters of pixels rather than smooth vector shapes
- readable silhouettes
- strong lighting and shadow
- subtle atmospheric depth

Do not make it look like normal vector art with a pixel filter applied afterward.

Avoid overly large pixels. There should be enough resolution to support many small details and expressive animation.

## Animation

The scene must feel continuously alive.

Do not animate everything at the same rhythm.

Use a mixture of:

### Continuous subtle motion

Examples:

- leaves gently swaying
- candle flames flickering
- hanging herbs moving slightly
- bubbles rising inside bottles
- steam drifting upward
- particles floating through the air
- water gently moving
- glowing plants softly pulsing
- insects hovering
- moonlight subtly shifting
- the cat breathing while asleep

### Periodic motion

Examples:

- the cat moves its tail
- a flower opens slightly
- a book page turns by itself
- a mushroom releases spores
- a bottle bubbles more strongly
- a hanging object swings
- a moth flies near a lamp

### Random events

Add several small events that happen at unpredictable intervals.

Examples:

- a magical flower suddenly glows
- a tiny spirit briefly appears and disappears
- one bottle flashes
- a plant blinks or reacts
- a shooting star crosses the window
- the cat looks up and then goes back to sleep
- a mysterious silhouette passes outside
- a few glowing spores burst into the air
- a magical rune activates briefly
- a tiny creature runs across a shelf

Random events should be relatively rare so they feel surprising.

The viewer should be able to leave the animation running and occasionally notice something new happening.

Avoid making the animation feel like an obvious short repeating loop.

## Lighting

Lighting is extremely important.

Use at least three different light influences:

- warm interior candle or lamp light
- cool moonlight coming through the window
- colored magical light emitted by plants, liquids, or artifacts

These light sources should interact with nearby objects.

Use reflections, illuminated glass, glowing leaves, subtle shadows, and contrasting warm/cool regions to make the scene visually rich.

## Exterior world

The window should show enough of the outside environment to make the world feel larger than the room.

Include some combination of:

- night sky
- moon
- stars
- distant trees
- mountains
- clouds
- rain
- fireflies
- distant lights

The exterior should also contain subtle animation.

## Interaction between elements

Whenever possible, animations should influence nearby elements rather than behaving independently.

Examples:

- magical light briefly illuminates nearby leaves
- passing moonlight changes the silhouette of plants
- a glowing insect creates a tiny moving light
- bubbles disturb the surface of liquid
- the cat reacts to a floating magical creature
- spores drift according to an implied air current

These interactions should make the world feel internally connected.

## Technical requirements

Create the complete result as a self-contained web experience.

Use only:

- HTML
- CSS
- JavaScript

Deliver the entire experience in a single `index.html` file.

Do not use external images, videos, fonts, frameworks, libraries, APIs, or network requests.

Everything visible must be generated with HTML, CSS, Canvas, SVG, or JavaScript contained within the file.

The result should:

- run immediately when the HTML file is opened
- fill the available browser viewport
- remain visually coherent across common desktop screen sizes
- animate smoothly
- avoid excessive CPU usage
- contain no UI panels, instructions, buttons, debug overlays, or explanatory text

The artwork itself should occupy the experience.

## Creative freedom

Do not treat the examples above as a checklist that must be represented literally.

Compose them into one coherent world.

You may invent additional magical plants, creatures, botanical experiments, environmental effects, and small narrative moments.

Prioritize:

1. visual impact
2. atmosphere
3. richness of detail
4. believable motion
5. surprising random events
6. pixel-art quality
7. the feeling that the world continues existing even when the viewer is not watching

The final result should feel less like a technical demo and more like a small living world.

Create the finished `index.html` now.
